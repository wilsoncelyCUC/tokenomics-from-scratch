# Research and Essays

The trends and the business view. Read these for judgment, not for today's prices.

## Prices fall, bills rise: read these three together

**[Epoch AI: LLM inference prices have fallen rapidly but unequally across tasks](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/llm-inference-price-trends)** (March 2025) · *Job: forecast your AI cost next year.*
Reaching the same quality got 9× to 900× cheaper per year, depending on the task. GPT-4-level science answers got 40× cheaper per year.
Why trust it: Epoch is an independent research group; the data and method are public.

**[a16z: Welcome to LLMflation](https://a16z.com/llmflation-llm-inference-cost/)** (November 2024) · *Job: explain the trend in one sentence to your board.*
For the same capability, the price drops about 10× every year: what cost $60 per million tokens in 2021 cost $0.06 in 2024.
Why trust it: simple, widely quoted, and the author says himself the pace may not last.

**[Ethan Ding: Tokens are getting more expensive](https://ethanding.substack.com/p/ai-subscriptions-get-short-squeezed)** (August 2025) · *Job: understand why your AI bill grows anyway.*
Each token gets cheaper, but people always want the newest model and each task now uses far more tokens. Flat-price subscriptions get squeezed.
Why it matters: the counterweight to the two above. Cheaper tokens do not mean a cheaper product.

## Agents: what they cost and when they are worth it

**[Anthropic: How we built our multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)** (June 2025) · *Job: budget an agent feature.*
Agents used about 4× the tokens of a chat; multi-agent systems about 15×. In their evaluation, how many tokens were spent explained 80% of the difference in quality.
Why it matters: real production numbers from a model maker. Use agents only where the task is worth the spend.

**[Anthropic: Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)** (December 2024) · *Job: decide whether you need an agent at all.*
Agents trade cost and speed for better results. Start with the simplest thing that works: one model call, then a fixed workflow, and only then an agent.
Why it matters: the most cited practical guide on the topic, and a cost argument as much as a design one.
