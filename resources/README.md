# Resources

The few things worth your time on AI economics: tools you can run, research you can quote, data you can plug in.
Written for finance leads, product managers and program managers who need to understand the numbers before they sign off on an AI feature.

Short on purpose. Every entry answers one job you have, and earned its place because the community uses it or the research holds up.

## Start here: five picks, five jobs

| Your job | Read or run | What you walk away with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Know if AI gets cheaper over time | [Epoch AI: inference price trends](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/llm-inference-price-trends) | The same quality gets 9× to 900× cheaper per year, depending on the task. Plan for it. |
| Know why your bill still goes up | [Tokens are getting more expensive](https://ethanding.substack.com/p/ai-subscriptions-get-short-squeezed) | Cheaper tokens, but each task now uses far more of them. Flat pricing gets squeezed. |
| Budget an agent feature | [Anthropic: our multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system) | A multi-agent system used about 15× the tokens of a chat. Only worth it for high-value tasks. |
| Pay less without losing quality | [FrugalGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176) | Try a cheap model first, escalate only when needed: matched GPT-4 at up to 98% less cost. |
| See and cap what each team spends | [LiteLLM](https://github.com/BerriAI/litellm) | One front door for every model call, with budgets per team and a spend log. |

## How an entry gets in

It must do at least one of these, for a reader who is not a full-time engineer:

- help you estimate what an AI task really costs
- help you control or cut that cost
- help you decide between a cheap and an expensive model
- explain a trend you will need to defend in a budget meeting

And it must be trusted: widely used (stars, adoption), peer-reviewed, or from a team that measured it in production.
